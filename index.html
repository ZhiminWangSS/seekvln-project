01 / The problemProgress myopia

Confidence
isn’t evidence.

Imagine being told to turn toward a landmark you cannot see. Thinking harder about the same view won’t reveal what’s around the corner.

Navigation agents can face the same problem. They may confidently keep moving even when the visual evidence needed to understand their progress is missing. We call this failure mode Progress Myopia.

SeekVLN gives the agent another option: acquire task-relevant observations before committing to its next action.

The instruction

“Stop in front of the chair.”

The missing link

The chair is outside the current view.

The useful action

Look for evidence. Then move.

02 / The approachLearn to seek. Learn when it helps.

A policy that learns
when to look.

At each decision, SeekVLN chooses between navigating directly and seeking additional views. When it seeks, it identifies the evidence, reasons about completed and upcoming subgoals, and then acts.

I.
FRG / Supervised fine-tuning

Learn what
to look for.

Future-guided Reverse Generation works backward from future expert actions. It enriches offline trajectories with supplementary views and evidence annotations, establishing a prior for seeking and progress reasoning.

Offline demonstrations → Evidence-seeking supervision
II.
C2PO / Reinforcement fine-tuning

Learn whether
looking helped.

Counterfactual Contrastive Policy Optimization compares two branches from the same state: seek evidence, or navigate directly. Their difference in subsequent progress provides credit for useful seeking decisions.

Same state → Two branches → Contrastive reward

04 / A closer lookSelective, purposeful observation

It’s not about
looking more often.

It’s about looking when the evidence matters. On a 100-episode R2R-CE subset, adaptive seeking outperforms both never seeking and seeking every two steps—even though it seeks less often than the periodic strategy.
