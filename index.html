01 / AbstractSeekVLN

Abstract

Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) requires agents to continuously ground task progress from long-horizon instructions and partial egocentric observations. Existing VLM-based navigation agents typically reason only over available observations and may remain confident even when task-relevant evidence is missing. We term this failure mode Progress Myopia: the agent fails to recognize unreliable progress grounding and continues acting on insufficient evidence.

We propose SeekVLN, an evidence-seeking framework that couples semantic progress reasoning with active acquisition of task-relevant observations. Future-guided Reverse Generation (FRG) augments offline expert trajectories with supplementary views and evidence annotations, establishing an initial behavior prior through supervised fine-tuning. Counterfactual Contrastive Policy Optimization (C2PO) then compares evidence-seeking and direct-navigation branches from the same state, assigning credit to seeking decisions according to their subsequent navigation benefit.

On the validation-unseen splits, SeekVLN improves success rate over the base model by 12.7 percentage points on R2R-CE and 7.5 percentage points on RxR-CE. Simulated and real-world qualitative rollouts further demonstrate targeted evidence-seeking behavior for more reliable progress grounding.

02 / MotivationProgress myopia

Progress Myopia

Imagine being told to turn toward a landmark you cannot see. Thinking harder about the same view won’t reveal what’s around the corner.

Navigation agents can face the same problem. They may confidently keep moving even when the visual evidence needed to understand their progress is missing. We call this failure mode Progress Myopia.

SeekVLN gives the agent another option: acquire task-relevant observations before committing to its next action.

The instruction

“Stop in front of the chair.”

The missing link

The chair is outside the current view.

The useful action

Look for evidence. Then move.

Method Overview

At each decision, SeekVLN chooses between navigating directly and seeking additional views. When it seeks, it identifies the evidence, reasons about completed and upcoming subgoals, and then acts.

Qualitative Results

In simulation, supplementary views reveal a dining area or a hallway beyond a kitchen island. On the Unitree Go2, a side view locates a chair missing from the forward observation.

The physical-robot rollout is a qualitative demonstration; it does not establish a real-world benchmark success rate.
